Before work begins

Confirm what exists before new equipment depends on it.

One home has no room set aside for work. Another has an office, studio or hobby room, where equipment may be concentrated, often on a single circuit. In either, a spare outlet or unused wire is not proof it suits new equipment.

List what already exists before you buy anything: the spare circuit, outlet or rough-in you hope to use, and which room it serves. Photograph labels and finished surfaces from outside only, and leave every cover and device untouched.

Which records show what is already installed in my home?

Look for earlier ESA paperwork, inspection reports, drawings from past renovations and seller disclosures. They describe what was done, not whether it still suits new equipment, so a Licensed Electrical Contractor should still assess it.

Can a spare circuit be used for new equipment as it is?

Not without an assessment. A circuit left unused may have been set up for a different purpose, and its route and condition are not visible from the room. Ask the contractor to state in writing what it can carry.

Why do two quotes disagree about which existing wiring is kept?

Check whether each written scope says what existing wiring or equipment is being kept and what is being replaced. Quotes that rely on the same provision should state the same assumption about it, so ask each contractor to explain any difference.

Who needs to agree before an existing outlet or circuit is reused?

The person who can authorize changes to the property should agree, and a rental, condominium or shared building may need a landlord or corporation to approve as well. Note who can open the door when the contractor visits.

After I send the form, who gives me a price for the work?

Any price comes from a provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor. The site publishes guides, stays out of visits and pricing and plays no part in the electrical work. Enquiries are received, then reviewed. A provider you choose shows its licence and identity separately, worth checking with ESA first; scope and cost are agreed after that, as is timing.
